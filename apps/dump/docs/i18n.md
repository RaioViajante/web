# Dump: English and Brazilian Portuguese

What Dump has implemented of the multilingual contract in
[docs/i18n.md](../../../docs/i18n.md). The shared document owns the rules every
app follows; this one records Dump's own state and decisions. Editorial rules for
the posts themselves are in [content/README.md](../content/README.md).

## Coverage

Every work exists in both languages: the twelve English originals have a
Portuguese version, and the Portuguese original
(`traduzir-o-site-sem-fingir-que-tudo-ja-existe`) has an English one. All 28
tags have a Portuguese label and slug. `tests/coverage-matrix.test.ts` pins this
per `contentId`, and `tests/translation-parity.test.ts` checks that a
translation contains everything its original contains (headings, code, links,
directives), in the same order.

Coverage is a fact of the content, not a requirement: a work with no
translation is valid, and its Portuguese route then does not exist.

## Publication dates

**A translation is not a publication.** Every version of a work, in any
language, carries the `publishedAt` of the original, also when the original is
Portuguese and the English version came later.

- `validatePostCollection` (`lib/posts.ts`) fails the build when the versions of
  one `contentId` disagree on `publishedAt`, in the same pass that checks
  `locale`, `originalLocale` and provenance. `tests/content-identity.test.ts`
  covers the rule, including a draft translation; `tests/coverage-matrix.test.ts`
  pins each work's original date for both locales.
- Each original's date equals the UTC date of the commit that first published it
  (checked for all thirteen works). Dates are never taken from the build, from
  Git at build time or from the time a translation went live.
- The consequences follow from the shared contract: `datePublished`,
  `article:published_time` and the RSS `pubDate` of a translation are the
  original's date; the Portuguese order is the English order, with ties on one
  day broken alphabetically by title; the Portuguese original is the newest
  Portuguese post and the English translation of it the newest English post.
- `updatedAt` is only ever an explicit, material update, written by hand and
  never derived from Git, the build or the clock. It feeds `dateModified`,
  `article:modified_time` and the sitemap `lastmod`, and nothing else does. A
  recorded date may be verified from the commit that made the update (author
  date in UTC); the evidence goes in the PR. Every version of an updated work
  carries the same date. Two works have one: `split-my-internet-...`
  (English and Portuguese) and `traduzir-o-site-...` (Portuguese and English),
  both 2026-10-30 (see below); no other version has an `updatedAt`.
- The earlier rule ("date each translation when it goes public", with the
  placeholder `2026-10-29`) is withdrawn. `traduzir-o-site-...` and its English
  translation say so in a marked update note.

## Statements in older posts that time has overtaken

A post is the author's text at the date it was written. When a later change
makes a sentence false, the sentence is put in the past tense and a short note
(`:::note[Atualização]` in Portuguese, `:::note[Update]` in English) states what
is true now, in both languages. History is not rewritten as if the feature had
existed earlier, and the note is never presented as part of the original.

| Post                                                   | What was overtaken                                                                                                 | What the note says                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `traduzir-o-site-...` and its English translation      | The "first stage" of the rollout: Portuguese participation off, no `/pt/rss.xml`, no comments on Portuguese texts. | Participation is on; `/pt/rss.xml` exists and is advertised; Portuguese texts have comments, one discussion per work shared by both languages. |
| The same two files                                     | "The translation does not inherit the original's date."                                                            | The rule changed: every version carries the original's `publishedAt`.                                                                          |
| `split-my-internet-...` and its Portuguese translation | IBM Plex Mono and Source Serif 4 as the shared typefaces (true when written, 2026-09-13).                          | The sites now share one self-hosted typeface, Noto Sans Mono.                                                                                  |

The four updated versions carry `updatedAt: 2026-10-30`, verified from the
commits that made the updates: `0d4fc90` (2026-10-30T03:19:55Z, the past-tense
wording and the notes on the rollout and the typefaces) and `ef4f9c6`
(2026-10-30T04:47:19Z, the note on "separate repositories", in the ecosystem
post only). `publishedAt` is untouched. The English `split-my-internet-...`
had 2026-10-05 recorded in production for an earlier edit; the later update
replaces it as the latest material update. The tests that pin this are in
`tests/verified-updates.ts`.

## Marking English inside Portuguese posts

Posts are compiled without a components map, so the only raw markup a post may
contain is the inline `<span lang="en">…</span>` that
[docs/i18n.md](../../../docs/i18n.md) prescribes for foreign-language fragments.
`tests/language-annotations.test.ts` allows that span and nothing else in prose,
requires it to be balanced, and forbids it in headings and around code, paths or
identifiers. The search index strips the tags.

Annotated: titles of works and milestones written in English (the series
_Building an OS_, the _Compiler Foundation_ milestone) and short English phrases
the text quotes or explains ("end line", "jump").

Not annotated, on purpose:

- code, inline code, commands, file names, API identifiers and project names;
- English technical terms used as loanwords by Brazilian developers (deadline,
  retry, commit, parser, pacing, event sourcing, dirty checking, Direction Flag,
  Zero Flag), which read as Portuguese in context;
- frontmatter (`title`, `description`), which has no inline markup. The
  Portuguese title "Dei mais de um deadline..." carries the loanword _deadline_.

## Feeds and GUIDs

`/rss.xml` and `/pt/rss.xml` list their own locale's published versions. The
twelve legacy English items keep `guid === link`. The one English item that did
not exist before (the translation of the Portuguese original) uses the
non-permalink tag GUID from the shared contract,
`tag:dump.raioviajante.com,2026:post/<contentId>/en` with
`isPermaLink="false"`. `seo/metadata-policy.ts` accepts exactly that shape in
addition to `guid === link` and rejects any other mismatch; the policy is not
wider than that.

## Legal pages

`/pt/terms` and `/pt/privacy` are faithful translations of the English pages:
same sections, facts, CNPJ and contact, no clause added or removed; English
remains the authoritative text. The English pages carry no last-updated date, so
the translations carry none. They are in the route registry, the sitemap and the
`LANG` counterparts, and the Portuguese footer links them without the "em inglês"
qualifier. They are not in the search index, which holds posts only. The
Portuguese text has not had a legal review; see the PR for the section-by-section
comparison.

## Search suggestions

`/pt/search` passes explicit suggestions (`lib/search-suggestions.ts`). Each term
must find a Portuguese entry; `tests/search-suggestions.test.ts` runs them
through the real index. The "everywhere" terms depend on the Portuguese content
of Root, Docs and Lab and are clicked through in
`e2e/dump-locale-navigation.spec.ts`.

## Client code

Locale data stays on the server. The page catalog (`lib/copy.ts`) is read by
server components only; the one client component, `Comments`, uses the small
`lib/comments-copy.ts`. Importing the catalog there added about 1.7 KB gzip of
unused copy to every article page, and `tests/client-bundle.test.ts` now fails
if a client component imports it.
